Why This Letter

Straight answers, before anything else.

We know a letter like this can be unsettling. Here's exactly what's going on.

It's a private notice

The letter came from The Permit Closer, a permit expediting company — not from your city, county, or any government office.

It's based on public records

We identify properties with unresolved permits using permit and property records maintained by the county — information that's a matter of public record.

It's informational, not urgent

Nothing is legally required of you right now. We're letting you know an issue exists so you can deal with it on your own terms, before it becomes a problem.

Why It's Worth Resolving

An unresolved permit doesn't go away on its own.

It stays on record until it's closed — and it tends to surface at the worst possible time.

  • Can delay or derail a future home sale during title search
  • Can complicate homeowners insurance coverage or claims
  • Can create code-enforcement exposure and potential fines
  • Often takes longer to resolve the longer it's left open

FAQ

Questions people ask us after getting this letter.

Is this letter a scam? +
No. The Permit Closer is a real permit expediting company and a division of Majestic Permits. The letter is a private notice, not a government document, and it's clearly identified as coming from our company.
Is this from the city or county? +
No. We are not a government agency and are not affiliated with your city or county building department. We are a private company that helps homeowners resolve permit issues with the jurisdiction.
How did you get my name and address? +
We identify properties with unresolved permits using publicly available county permit and property records. This information is a matter of public record.
Do I have to do anything right now? +
No immediate action is legally required by our letter alone. However, an unresolved permit can create complications later, particularly if you sell, refinance, or insure the property.
What happens if I ignore this letter? +
The permit will remain open or expired on county record. This can surface during a future title search, sale, refinance, or insurance review, and may require resolution at that point, often under time pressure.
How much does this cost? +
We don't publish pricing — every case is quoted individually based on the property and permit history. Call or request a free case review for an exact number.
Can I resolve this myself instead? +
Yes. You're free to contact your local building department directly. We offer this service for homeowners who would rather have someone manage the research and coordination for them.
